1 Structure enables trust
Technical foundation

AI becomes trustworthy when every answer traces back to structured, verifiable evidence. Without a trail from conclusion to source, no regulated industry will deploy AI at scale. The foundation of trust is better evidence chains, not better models.

2 Graphs beat documents
Technical foundation

Knowledge graphs store meaning as explicit connections, letting AI find related information that shares no words with the query. Vector-only RAG hits roughly 50% accuracy. Adding graph-based retrieval pushes that above 99%.

3 Semantic layer required
Organizational approach

The same word means different things to different departments. AI systems without explicit definitions of business terms produce inconsistent answers. A semantic layer translates between business language and technical data storage.

4 Reasoning constrains AI
Technical foundation

LLMs guess. Reasoning engines prove. Combining both gives the flexibility of natural language with the reliability of formal logic. Rules in DataLog or SHACL run automatically, catch violations before they reach users, and produce deterministic, auditable results.

5 Agents orchestrate, not answer
Technical foundation

The best architectures treat the LLM as a planner, not the source of truth. The agent decomposes questions, calls tools (graph queries, rule engines, databases), and assembles verified results. The LLM contributes language. The graph contributes facts.

6 Context differentiates
Organizational approach

When every organization has the same AI models, context is the differentiator: your terminology, unwritten rules, customer patterns, and edge cases. 56% of CEOs report zero financial benefit from AI because intelligence without context produces wrong answers.

7 Problem before technology
Organizational approach

Every successful KGC implementation started with a specific, measurable business problem. Every failed one started with "we need a knowledge graph." Technology-first approaches produce unfocused experiments. Problem-first approaches produce measurable results.

8 Human oversight remains
People and governance

No session at KGC advocated fully autonomous AI. Every architecture includes human validation, especially early on. AI handles volume and speed. Humans handle judgment and domain knowledge. The goal is augmenting expertise, not replacing it.

9 Governance is architecture
People and governance

Governance in documents gets ignored. Governance in the system gets enforced. The most effective approaches encode business rules, access controls, and validation directly into infrastructure. Compliance becomes automatic rather than aspirational.

10 Adoption is a people problem
People and governance

Technical migration at one case study finished six months before go-live. The delay was entirely human. Ontology engineers who do not use their own tools cannot explain their value to business users. Projects stall when builders are disconnected from users.
